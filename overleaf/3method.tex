This section formulates the methodology.

\subsection{Gradient Flow Analysis} \label{sec:gradientAnalysis}

\subsection{Multi-Fidelity Gradient Estimation} \label{sec:mf-gradient}
As previously analyzed, no analytic gradient of $f_{n,k}$ is available, so the gradient must be estimated from sampling data alone. We pair two estimators according to the cost of their underlying oracles: a one-point bandit estimator for the high-fidelity dynamic model, whose evaluations are expensive, and a deterministic two-point estimator for the low-fidelity quasi-static model, whose evaluations are cheap.

Based on \eqref{eq:cost_sum}, the partial derivative of $f_k$ with respect to $\gamma_{n,k}$ decomposes into an own term and a set of coupling terms,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial f_k}{\partial \gamma_{n,k}}
    = \frac{\partial f_{n,k}}{\partial \gamma_{n,k}}
    + \sum_{m \in \mathcal{N}_n} \frac{\partial f_{m,k}}{\partial \gamma_{n,k}},
    \label{eq:coord}
\end{equation}
where $\sum_{m \in \mathcal{N}_n} \partial f_{m,k}/\partial \gamma_{n,k}$ represents the aggregate sensitivity of the neighboring turbines' costs to the yaw action of FOWT$_n$, i.e., the wake-induced coupling. We assign each term to the fidelity that can estimate it reliably.

\emph{One-point bandit estimator (high fidelity).} Querying a single perturbed point,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:one-bandit}
    \hat{g}_{n,k} := \frac{1}{\delta_k} f_{n,k}\!\left(\gamma_{n,k}+\delta_k u_{n,k},\,\{\gamma_{m,k}\}_{m\in\mathcal{N}_n}\right) u_{n,k},
\end{equation}
where $u_{n,k} \in \{-1,+1\}$ is drawn uniformly at random and $\delta_k > 0$ is an exploration parameter. Since each yaw $\gamma_{n,k}$ is scalar, no sphere sampling is required, and the committed point is never queried.

\emph{Two-point estimator (low fidelity).} Because low-fidelity evaluations are inexpensive, the low-fidelity gradient is formed by a deterministic central difference that removes the one-point sampling variance,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:two-point}
    \hat{g}^{l}_{n,k} := \frac{1}{2\delta_k}\Big( f^{l}_{n,k}(\gamma_{n,k}+\delta_k) - f^{l}_{n,k}(\gamma_{n,k}-\delta_k) \Big),
\end{equation}
keeping every other yaw fixed at its committed value.

\emph{Wake-convection delay and dwell time.} In a dynamic high-fidelity model, perturbing the yaw of FOWT$_m$ does not reach a downstream FOWT$_n$ instantaneously: the modified wake must first convect over the turbine spacing. This wake-convection delay is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:tau-conv}
    \tau_{\text{conv}} \approx \frac{d_{m,n}}{\bar{u}},
\end{equation}
where $d_{m,n}$ is the streamwise turbine spacing and $\bar{u}$ the wake-advection speed. We dwell on each perturbation for a time $D$ selected so that the local (own) response has settled while the cross response has not yet arrived,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:dwell}
    T_{\text{settle}} < D < \tau_{\text{conv}}.
\end{equation}
Within this window the own power of FOWT$_n$ reflects only its own yaw perturbation. Hence, if all turbines are perturbed \emph{simultaneously}, the own response of FOWT$_n$ is identical to the case in which only $\gamma_{n,k}$ is perturbed, since the neighboring perturbations have not yet reached FOWT$_n$. This observation is the key enabler of the proposed estimator: the delayed cross response, which precludes a direct high-fidelity estimate of the coupling, is precisely what renders the simultaneous own-response probe clean.

\emph{Per-term multi-fidelity estimator.} The own term $\partial f_{n,k}/\partial\gamma_{n,k}$ is estimated from the high-fidelity dynamic model via the simultaneous one-point probe, whereas each coupling term $\partial f_{m,k}/\partial\gamma_{n,k}$, $m\in\mathcal{N}_n$, is estimated from the low-fidelity quasi-static model via the deterministic two-point estimator. The resulting multi-fidelity estimator is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mf-gradient}
    \hat{g}_{n,k} = \hat{g}^{h}_{n,n,k} + \sum_{m\in\mathcal{N}_n}\hat{g}^{l}_{m,n,k},
\end{equation}
where, following \eqref{eq:one-bandit} and \eqref{eq:two-point},
\begin{equation} \label{eq:own-hf}
    \hat{g}^{h}_{n,n,k} = \frac{1}{\delta_k} f^{h}_{n,k}\!\left(\gamma_{n,k}+\delta_k u_{n,k},\,\{\gamma_{m,k}\}_{m\in\mathcal{N}_n};\,D\right) u_{n,k},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation} \label{eq:cross-lf}
    \hat{g}^{l}_{m,n,k} = \frac{1}{2\delta_k}\Big( f^{l}_{m,k}(\gamma_{n,k}+\delta_k) - f^{l}_{m,k}(\gamma_{n,k}-\delta_k) \Big).
\end{equation}
Here the superscripts $h$ and $l$ denote the high- and low-fidelity models, and $f^{h}_{n,k}(\cdot\,;D)$ denotes the high-fidelity power measured after the dwell $D$. This estimator is biased only by the $O(\delta_k^2)$ finite-difference error and by the low-fidelity modeling error of the coupling terms, under the standing assumption
\begin{equation} \label{eq:coupling-assumption}
    \frac{\partial f^{l}_{m,k}}{\partial\gamma_{n,k}} \approx \frac{\partial f^{h}_{m,k}}{\partial\gamma_{n,k}}, \qquad m\in\mathcal{N}_n.
\end{equation}
Unlike a variance-reducing control variate, the low-fidelity model is not merely a correction to the high-fidelity estimate; it supplies the coupling terms that the dynamic model cannot provide within the dwell window.

\subsection{Proposed Algorithm} \label{sec:algorithm}
We now design the distributed online algorithm with multi-fidelity bandit feedback. To ensure practical feasibility, perturbation and gradient estimation are coordinated as follows. At each time step $k$, all turbines are perturbed simultaneously by $\Gamma_k + \delta_k u_k$, and each turbine measures its own power after the dwell $D$ to form the high-fidelity own-partial estimate~\eqref{eq:own-hf}. Concurrently, each FOWT$_n$ evaluates the farm-wide low-fidelity model by perturbing only its own yaw and reading the coupling-term costs $\{f^{l}_{m,k}\}_{m\in\mathcal{N}_n}$ from the resulting low-fidelity cost vector, yielding the cross-partial estimates~\eqref{eq:cross-lf} without any high-fidelity cross-measurement. The own and coupling partials are then assembled as in \eqref{eq:mf-gradient}.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Cooperative Distributed Online Feedback Optimization Based on Multi-Fidelity Gradient Estimator.}
\label{alg:doo-mf}
\begin{algorithmic}
    \STATE \textbf{Input:} stepsize $\{\alpha_k\}$; exploration $\{\delta_k\}$; shrinkage $\{\xi_k\} \subset (0,1)$ with $\delta_k \le r(X_n)\xi_k$; dwell $D$ with $T_{\text{settle}} < D < \tau_{\text{conv}}$.
    \STATE \textbf{Initialize:} $\gamma_{n,1} \in (1-\xi_1)X_n$ for all $n \in [N]$ (one sign quadrant fixed).
    \FOR{$k = 1, 2, \dots$}
        \STATE Draw $u_k=(u_{1,k},\dots,u_{N,k}) \in \{-1,+1\}^N$ uniformly at random; command all turbines to $\Gamma_k+\delta_k u_k$ simultaneously and dwell for $D$.
        \FOR{$n = 1, \dots, N$ in parallel}
            \STATE Measure own power after the dwell; form the own-partial estimate $\hat{g}^{h}_{n,n,k}$ via \eqref{eq:own-hf}.
            \STATE For each $m \in \mathcal{N}_n$, evaluate the low-fidelity model at $\gamma_{n,k} \pm \delta_k$ (all other yaws fixed); form the coupling-partial estimate $\hat{g}^{l}_{m,n,k}$ via \eqref{eq:cross-lf}.
            \STATE Assemble the multi-fidelity gradient $\hat{g}_{n,k} = \hat{g}^{h}_{n,n,k} + \sum_{m \in \mathcal{N}_n}\hat{g}^{l}_{m,n,k}$.
            \STATE Update $\gamma_{n,k+1} = \mathcal{P}_{(1-\xi_{k+1})X_n}\big(\gamma_{n,k} - \alpha_{k}\hat{g}_{n,k}\big)$.
        \ENDFOR
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE \textbf{Output:} $\{\gamma_{n,k}\}$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
