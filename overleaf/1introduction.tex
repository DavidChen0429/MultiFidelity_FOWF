\section{Introduction} \label{sec:introduction}
Floating offshore wind farm control aims to mitigate the wake effect and thereby improve the overall performance of the farm. One effective strategy is turbine repositioning, whereby floating wind turbines yaw and move themselves out of the wake to reduce wake losses. The traditional approach to this problem relies on a centralized, model-free architecture, which is not scalable and demands substantial communication as the number of turbines grows. Motivated by this, the present work develops a distributed online optimization method for floating offshore wind farm control.

Multi-agent coordination-based distributed online optimization is a powerful tool to solve wind farm control problems~\cite{li2023survey}. However, there are a few problems: first, many problems consider consensus-based distributed online optimization, which is not the case for wind farm control because we do not want wind turbines to converge to a single yaw. Second, a majority of existing methods assume convexity or even strong convexity of the cost functions as well as full gradient or subgradient information feedback. These assumptions do not hold for the wind farm control problem, which is non-convex and for which no analytic gradient is available. Moreover, due to the delay induced by the wake propagation, the influence of control action from the upstream wind turbine takes time to reach the downwind wind turbine, causing a time delay. Therefore, the main challenge is to address these three issues: non-convexity, the absence of gradient information, and the dynamic time delay. Therefore, the goal is to design an online optimization method that is based on a multi-agent coordination-based distributed online optimization framework, while addressing these three issues.
